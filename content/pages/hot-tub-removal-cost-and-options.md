An old hot tub is one of the hardest things to get rid of at home. Even empty, it weighs hundreds of pounds. It's usually wider than your gate. It's wired to its own circuit, and it's full of treated water you can't just dump in the street. This guide covers what removal costs according to published sources, the jobs you may need to do (or pay for) first, and each way to get it gone: haul-away, cutting it up for a dumpster, or giving it to someone who wants it.

> **Quick answer.** Angi and HomeAdvisor both put hot tub removal at **$150 to $800**, with a **$400** national average. In-ground tubs run **$400 to $1,100** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/)). Before any crew comes: (1) have a **licensed electrician** disconnect a hardwired tub. LoadUp says that typically costs **$200 to $500** ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)). (2) **Drain it** to the sanitary sewer or your lawn, not the storm drain, if your city says so (many do). (3) Decide between a **junk removal crew** (they cut it up and haul it) and **cutting it up yourself** into a 10 or 20 yard dumpster. If it still works, try **listing it free** first. Prices and rules vary, so confirm with your hauler and your city.

### What pushes the price up

- **Stairs and tight access.** Angi and HomeAdvisor say many movers add **$100 to $125** per job for stairs or limited access. HomeGuide says $100 to $130 for stairs ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/), [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/hot-tub-removal-cost)).
- **Cutting it apart.** A shell is one molded piece. LoadUp notes that standard gates run 32 to 36 inches wide, so a tub that won't fit gets cut into sections on site ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)). College HUNKS says hot tubs may cost extra because of disassembly time ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/pricing/junk-removal-pricing/)).
- **Size and weight.** HomeAdvisor puts standard tubs at **500 to 1,000 lb empty**, with larger models over 1,000 lb ([HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/)). Angi says a 2–3 person tub is about 500 lb empty and a six-person tub about 1,000 lb ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-dispose-of-hot-tub.htm)).
- **Truck space.** Volume-priced haulers charge by how much of the truck you fill. College HUNKS says a full truckload (about 440 cubic feet) holds about **2 standard-size hot tubs**, so one tub is roughly half a truck there ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/pricing/junk-removal-pricing/)). 1-800-GOT-JUNK also prices by volume, from 1/8 of a truck up to a full load ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-take/hot-tub-removal)).
- **Permits.** Some places require a permit, especially for in-ground removal. Angi and HomeAdvisor put permits at **$50 to $500** (HomeAdvisor's summary box says $150 to $500) ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/)).
- **Disposal not included.** Angi says most companies build disposal into the price, and HomeGuide says many do while others charge separately. Ask before you agree ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm), [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/hot-tub-removal-cost)).

## Step 1: Get the power disconnected (by a licensed electrician)

This is the step people skip, and it's the one that cancels pickups.

- **Hardwired tubs need an electrician.** HomeAdvisor says most units run on dedicated **220–240 volt** circuits and recommends a licensed electrician ([HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/)). LoadUp says a hardwired 240-volt tub must be disconnected by a licensed electrician, "not just switched off at the breaker," and that its crews won't do electrical, gas, or plumbing disconnection ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)).
- **Why the breaker isn't enough.** If the tub gets cut up on site, the saw is working near the wiring. Blue Sky Disposal, a Michigan dumpster company, says to have an electrician disconnect and cap the wiring at the shutoff box ([Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan)).
- **Plug-in tubs are simpler.** LoadUp says unplugging is enough for a 120-volt plug-and-play portable spa ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)).
- **Do it before pickup day.** LoadUp says if the tub is still connected, "the job can't move forward that day" ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)). Ask your hauler what they need. Some, like Junk King, ask you to turn off and disconnect the circuits at the breaker before the crew arrives ([Junk King](https://www.junk-king.com/services/items-we-take/hot-tubs)).

## Step 2: Drain it the legal way

A full tub can weigh "several thousand pounds" with the water in, so it must be empty first. LoadUp says to drain it at least a day ahead ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)). Where the water goes matters.

**The storm drain is usually off-limits.** Storm drains in many cities flow to creeks, rivers, or lakes without treatment. Some how-to guides, including one on Angi, suggest draining to a nearby storm drain ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-dispose-of-hot-tub.htm)). But plenty of cities ban it. Three real examples:

| City or agency | Rule |
|---|---|
| [Portland, OR (Environmental Services)](https://www.portland.gov/bes/preventing-pollution/prevent-pollution/drain-pools-and-hot-tubs) | City code prohibits heated, chlorinated, or salt-treated pool and hot tub water in the storm drain. Drain to the sanitary sewer or let it soak into the ground. Only as a last resort may dechlorinated water go to the storm system: cooled at least 12 hours, sat out at least two weeks to 0.1 mg/L chlorine or less, neutral pH, under 50 gallons per minute. Bromine, copper, and salt water are never allowed. Violations can bring fines |
| [West Valley Stormwater Authority (Campbell, Los Gatos, Monte Sereno, Saratoga, CA)](https://www.cleancreeks.org/292/Draining-Pools-Spas-and-Fountains) | Nothing into the street or storm drain. Dechlorinate first (5–10 days in the sun with no chlorine added, or a chemical dechlorinator) and test. For spas, hose into a sewer cleanout or an indoor drain like a bathtub. No cleanout? Drain onto your lawn if it can hold the water without running off. Don't drain into a septic system or a toilet |
| [City of North Las Vegas, NV](https://www.cityofnorthlasvegas.com/residents/water/water-conservation/pools-and-hot-tubs) | Unlawful to drain a pool, hot tub, or whirlpool bath into the street or storm drain where sanitary sewer is available. Use a sewer cleanout, sink, toilet, or bathtub. Violations can bring penalties |

**What this means for you:** Stop adding chemicals, let the chlorine or bromine drop, and test it. Then hose it into your sewer cleanout or an indoor drain, or onto your own lawn if your city allows it. Search "[your city] drain hot tub" or call your public works or sewer department. Rules differ, so confirm with your city. On a septic system, ask before draining to an indoor drain (the West Valley agency warns against septic).

### Option B: Cut it up yourself and rent a dumpster

This makes sense if the tub is part of a bigger job, like tearing out the deck or gazebo around it. Angi says a dumpster may be more cost-effective when removal is part of a bigger renovation ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-dispose-of-hot-tub.htm)).

**What size dumpster for a hot tub?** Published hauler suggestions:

| Project | Suggested size | Source |
|---|---|---|
| One hot tub, cut up | 10 yard | [Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan) |
| Hot tub plus steps, cover, and gazebo | 20 yard | [Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan) |

| Small / medium / large tubs | 10 / 15 / 20 yard | [Nova Dumpsters (VA, DC, MD)](https://www.novadumpsters.com/hot-tub-removal-dumpster-rental/) |
| Concrete pad under the tub | Its own heavy-debris box, typically 10 yard | [Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan) |

**Weight:** The tub itself is fine. Blue Sky Disposal says an empty tub's 500 to 1,000 lb is "well within the included weight on most dumpsters" ([Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan)). **The concrete pad is the problem.** EPA's conversion table puts loose concrete scrap at **1,855 lb per cubic yard** ([U.S. EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-03/documents/conversions.pdf)). A few yards of broken pad can use up a general-debris box's weight allowance fast. Tell the hauler about concrete up front and ask for a heavy-material box. See [Dumpster rental sizes explained](/dumpster-rental-sizes-explained/) for allowances and [How much does a dumpster rental cost?](/how-much-does-a-dumpster-rental-cost/) for overage fees.

**What the DIY job looks like** (from [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-dispose-of-hot-tub.htm) and [Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan)):

1. Power disconnected and capped by an electrician, and the tub drained (Steps 1 and 2).
2. Remove the cover, steps, side panels, and loose pumps, heaters, and control boxes. Keep metal parts separate; a scrap yard may take copper wiring, pumps, and motors.
3. Cut the cabinet frame, then the shell, with a reciprocating saw. Blue Sky suggests strips about 2 to 3 feet wide that two people can carry.
4. Wear safety glasses, gloves, hearing protection, and a dust mask, and lay down a tarp for foam and fiberglass dust.
5. Load big flat pieces first and keep everything below the top edge of the dumpster.

Angi estimates cutting takes about **2.5 hours** for a smaller tub and **around 4 hours** for a larger one with one or two helpers ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-dispose-of-hot-tub.htm)). Blue Sky plans a full day for a 4–6 person tub. A dumpster parked on the street may need a city permit ([Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan)).

### Option C: Sell it or give it away

If the tub still works, this can cost you nothing. HomeGuide, Angi, HomeAdvisor, and LoadUp all suggest selling or listing it free first ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/hot-tub-removal-cost), [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/), [LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)).

- **Say who moves it.** HomeAdvisor suggests stating clearly in the listing that the buyer must handle removal.
- **Expect no-shows.** LoadUp warns that the person taking it needs a way to move several hundred pounds, "which is why plenty of interested people never actually follow through." A tub that leaks or hasn't run in years is much harder to give away.
- **Moving it costs money too.** If a buyer hires a hot tub mover, Angi puts relocation on the same property at **$150 to $200** and to a new house at **$350 or more** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm)).
- **Disclose problems.** HomeAdvisor advises telling buyers and recipients about the unit's condition and known issues.

### What about city bulky pickup?

Don't count on it. Northglenn, Colorado, lists hot tubs among items it **can't** collect in its special pickup ([City of Northglenn](https://www.northglenn.org/services/trash_recycling/special_pickup.php)). Northern Recycling and Waste Services (Paradise, California) says hot tubs are "too large to collect for a Bulky Item" and points residents to a disposal facility where fees apply ([Northern Recycling](https://northernrecycling.biz/guide/hot-tub/)). Junk King notes residential trash service generally won't take one ([Junk King](https://www.junk-king.com/services/items-we-take/hot-tubs)). Check your city's list, but plan on another option.

| | Portable / above-ground | In-ground or built-in |

For a tub built into a deck, LoadUp says it "may need some structural work first." Describe the setup honestly when you ask for quotes so the price doesn't change on site.

## Which option should I pick?

- **It works and you can wait a few weeks:** list it free or cheap, buyer hauls.
- **Portable tub, no other project:** get two or three junk removal quotes and compare with an upfront online price.
- **Tub plus deck, gazebo, or other debris:** rent a 10–20 yard dumpster and cut it up, or have a crew do it.
- **In-ground, or sitting on a concrete pad you want gone:** get quotes from a demolition contractor, and plan a separate heavy-debris dumpster for the concrete.

## Prep checklist before pickup day

1. Book the electrician first. Confirm the tub is disconnected and the wiring capped.
2. Stop adding chemicals, then drain it the way your city allows. Use a shop vacuum for leftover water in the footwells.
3. Pull the cover, steps, and loose items, or ask if the crew takes them (1-800-GOT-JUNK takes covers).
4. Measure your gate and path. Tell the hauler about stairs, decks, slopes, or fences.
5. Ask in writing: is disassembly included? Is disposal included? Are there stair or access fees?
6. Get a written price before work starts, and don't pay a stranger cash up front. See [Junk removal and dumpster rental scams: red flags](/junk-removal-and-dumpster-rental-scams-red-flags/).

## FAQ

**How much does it cost to get rid of a hot tub?** National guides put it at **$150 to $800**, with a **$400** average, and **$400 to $1,100** for in-ground tubs ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/)). Add an electrician if it's hardwired. Confirm with your hauler.

**Do I need an electrician to remove a hot tub?** For a hardwired 240-volt tub, yes in practice. LoadUp requires a licensed electrician's disconnect before pickup and won't do it itself, and HomeAdvisor recommends one ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/)). A 120-volt plug-in spa just gets unplugged.

**Can I drain my hot tub into the street?** In many cities, no. Portland, North Las Vegas, and California's West Valley Stormwater Authority all say no storm drain or street, and point you to the sanitary sewer or your lawn ([Portland](https://www.portland.gov/bes/preventing-pollution/prevent-pollution/drain-pools-and-hot-tubs), [North Las Vegas](https://www.cityofnorthlasvegas.com/residents/water/water-conservation/pools-and-hot-tubs), [West Valley](https://www.cleancreeks.org/292/Draining-Pools-Spas-and-Fountains)). Check your city's rule.

**Can I put a whole hot tub in a dumpster?** Usually not. Blue Sky Disposal says most are too wide and tall to fit or load safely, so cut it up first ([Blue Sky Disposal](https://www.blueskydisposal.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-a-hot-tub-michigan)). A 10 yard box fits one cut-up tub by that hauler's guide.

**How much does an empty hot tub weigh?** About 500 to 1,000 lb for standard tubs, and more for big ones ([HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/swimming-pools-hot-tubs-and-saunas/hot-tub-removal-cost/)). HomeGuide says 400 to 1,000 lb ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/hot-tub-removal-cost)).

**Can a hot tub be recycled?** Parts of it. LoadUp says metal parts like the frame, pump, and wiring are recycled where local options exist. The fiberglass or acrylic shell and foam are harder to recycle and usually go to an approved waste facility ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/hot-tub-removal/)). 1-800-GOT-JUNK says hot tub covers can be recycled, though some facilities want them taken apart ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-take/hot-tub-removal)).

**Will any junk removal company take a hot tub?** Most large ones list hot tubs as an item they take. Rules on in-ground tubs, connected tubs, and full tubs vary. See [What junk removal companies won't take](/what-junk-removal-companies-wont-take/), and ask before you book.
