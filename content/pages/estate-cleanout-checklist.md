Clearing out the home of someone you loved is not a normal cleanout. Every drawer can stop you in your tracks. This guide breaks the job into small, ordered steps so you don't have to hold the whole thing in your head at once. It covers what to do first, when you're allowed to start removing things, how to sort, how to sell and donate, what to do with papers and hazardous items, and what the hauling costs according to published sources.

Go at the pace you can manage. Most of the steps below can wait a few days. The few that can't (securing the home, finding the will, and not giving anything away yet) come first.

> **Quick answer.** (1) **Secure the home and find the will before anything leaves the house.** (2) **Wait for legal authority.** In many states, the person named as executor can't sell, give away, or distribute estate property until the court issues Letters (see the probate section below). (3) **Sort room by room into Keep, Sell, Donate, and Junk** ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout), [College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/blog/estate-cleanout/)). (4) **Estate sale companies usually take a commission,** commonly cited at **35% to 40%** of gross sales ([Angi, citing ASEL](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm), [Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)). (5) **Full-service estate cleanouts** run **$275 to $4,000** in Angi's data, with a **$1,250** average ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)). A **full junk removal truckload** is **$600 to $800** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)), and a **30 or 40 yard dumpster** runs **$300 to $800** a week ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-dumpster-rental.htm)). These are national ranges, not quotes. Confirm locally.

*This page is general information, not legal or tax advice. Probate rules differ by state and by estate. If you have questions about your authority as executor, dividing property among heirs, or selling the house, talk to a local probate or estate attorney before you remove anything.*

## The checklist at a glance

| Phase | What happens | Can wait? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First days | Secure the home, mail, medications, IDs, and financial records. Find the will. Start one working file | No. Do these first |
| 2. Legal authority | Executor or administrator gets Letters from the court (or uses a small-estate process). Ask an attorney what you may do before then | Nothing leaves until this is clear |
| 3. Inventory and family | Photograph and list each room. Ask heirs what they want. Flag possible valuables for appraisal | Yes, take your time |
| 4. Sort | Keep, Sell, Donate, Junk, room by room. Pull papers to keep and papers to shred | Yes |
| 5. Sell | Estate sale company, auction, consignment, or private sale | Yes |
| 6. Donate | Habitat ReStore, Salvation Army, and other local charities. Get receipts | Yes |
| 7. Hazardous and sensitive items | Medications, paint and chemicals, documents with account numbers | Do medications early for safety |
| 8. Haul | Junk removal crew, dumpster, or both | Yes |
| 9. Clean and hand off | Deep clean, final sweep of closets and attic, close utilities, hand over keys | Last |

*Steps are drawn from the Iowa Attorney General, 1-800-GOT-JUNK, College HUNKS, and Angi guidance linked below. Order and timing vary by estate.*

## Phase 1: The first few days

You don't have to start sorting yet. The Iowa Attorney General's guide says plainly that "not every task must be completed immediately" and suggests starting with safety, final arrangements, and the documents needed to act for the estate ([Iowa Attorney General](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/for-consumers/general-consumer-information/life-events/death-of-a-loved-one)). Its first-days list includes:

- **Find the will, trust, and any funeral or estate-planning documents.**
- **Secure the home, vehicles, mail, medications, identification, financial records, and devices.**
- **Avoid rushed financial decisions** or paying unfamiliar bills from your own money.
- **Keep one working file.** Write down every call (date, name, organization, result), keep copies of bills and receipts, and keep personal and estate money separate.

If the house will sit empty, 1-800-GOT-JUNK suggests changing the locks, keeping any alarm active, and **telling the homeowners insurance company the home is unoccupied** ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout)). It also suggests a quick first walkthrough just for perishables in the fridge, jewelry, cash, and sentimental photos. That's not the full cleanout. It's only grabbing what would spoil or could go missing.

One thing that surprises families: a **power of attorney generally ends when the person dies.** The Iowa AG warns not to keep using the person's power of attorney, checks, debit card, or passwords as though they were living ([Iowa Attorney General](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/for-consumers/general-consumer-information/life-events/death-of-a-loved-one)).

## Phase 2: Don't give things away before there's legal authority

This is the step most often skipped, and it can cause real trouble. Being named executor in a will is not the same as having authority to act. A court usually has to appoint you first and issue a document called **Letters Testamentary** (if there's a will) or **Letters of Administration** (if there isn't).

What official sources say, as examples:

- **New York:** state law says an executor named in a will "has no power to dispose of any part of the estate" before letters are granted, except to pay reasonable funeral expenses, and may only take action "necessary to preserve it" ([N.Y. Estates, Powers & Trusts Law § 11-1.3](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EPT/11-1.3)).
- **California:** the court's required form for personal representatives says, "You should not spend any of the estate's money unless you have received permission from the court or have been advised to do so by an attorney," and that you should consult an attorney about sales of estate property. It also requires an **inventory and appraisal within four months** after Letters are first issued ([Judicial Council of California, Form DE-147](https://courts.ca.gov/sites/default/files/courts/default/2024-11/de147.pdf)).
- **Haulers say it too.** 1-800-GOT-JUNK's estate guide says to wait for Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration before removing or selling property in probate, because doing it early "could lead to disputes among heirs or complications with the court" ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout)). College HUNKS recommends calling an estate attorney before hauling anything away ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/blog/estate-cleanout/)).

Not every estate goes through full probate. Some property passes by trust, joint ownership, or beneficiary designation, and many states have a simpler small-estate process. Which applies is a question for a local attorney or your county probate court's self-help center. The California courts, for example, publish a [self-help guide to property after someone dies](https://www.selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/probate).

**Taking things for yourself, even small keepsakes, can count as a distribution.** Wait until the executor (with an attorney's advice) says it's okay, and write down who took what.

## Phase 3: Inventory, family, and valuables

Before anything is sold or hauled:

1. **Photograph and list each room.** 1-800-GOT-JUNK suggests a room-by-room inventory with photos of large items ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout)). Photos also help junk removal and cleanout companies quote the job ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)).
2. **Ask heirs what they want, in writing.** Schwab suggests walking through the home with family and recording what each person wants to keep, what will be sold, and what stays off the market ([Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)).
3. **Flag possible valuables for a professional.** Art, antiques, jewelry, and collections may be worth a qualified appraisal. Schwab points to appraisers certified through the International Society of Appraisers, the Appraisers Association of America, or the American Society of Appraisers ([Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)). Angi lists identifying assets at **$2,000 to $6,000** when hired out as a service ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)).

## Phase 4: Sort, one room at a time

The common method is four piles: **Keep, Sell, Donate, Junk** (some guides call it the four-box method) ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/blog/estate-cleanout/), [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)). A few things that make it easier:

- **One room at a time.** College HUNKS suggests moving through the home in sections ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/junk-removal/items-we-take/estate-cleanout/)).
- **Colored stickers** for each destination, so helpers and crews know what goes where ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout)).
- **Papers first.** Find wills, trusts, deeds, insurance policies, bank statements, birth certificates, and vehicle titles before the junk pile grows. College HUNKS suggests scanning important documents before the cleanout continues ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/blog/estate-cleanout/)).
- **Check pockets, books, and boxes.** Cash, jewelry, and photos turn up in unexpected places, which is why the quick sweep in Phase 1 matters.
- **Sentimental items can wait.** Set photos, letters, and keepsakes in a labeled box and decide later. Nobody needs to decide about a wedding album on a deadline.

Sorting first also saves money. Angi notes that labeling and sorting items in advance reduces the work, and the labor bill, for a cleanout company ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)).

## Phase 5: Selling (estate sale, auction, or private sale)

An estate sale is a public sale of a home's contents, usually over one or more days, at set prices ([Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)). Most professional estate sale companies are paid a percentage of what sells.

| What the source says | Commission or fee | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Typical commission | 35%–40% of gross proceeds | [Angi, citing the American Society of Estate Liquidators](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm) |
| "Normally" charged | 35%–40% of total sales; generally covers research, promotion, cleanup, and haul-away | [Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows) |
| Range cited by a directory | 25%–45%, with 45% the most common rate in its poll | [EstateSales.org](https://estatesales.org/how-much-do-estate-sale-companies-charge) |
| Minimum estate size | Some companies want at least $10,000 in expected sales | [Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows) |

What's included varies a lot. The American Society of Estate Liquidators says a company may charge a commission, flat fee, hourly rate, minimum, expenses, or a mix. Some leave unsold items at the property for you to handle, and others will pack, donate, or remove them for an extra fee ([ASEL](https://www.aselonline.com/institute/consumer-tips-resources/articles-for-consumers/estate-sale-services-what-do-they-include/)). ASEL says a **written agreement** should spell out both sides' responsibilities, the services, and all fees.

Before you sign:

- **Meet two or three companies.** Check reviews and ask whether they're insured and bonded ([Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)).
- **Ask what happens to unsold items** and whether the post-sale cleanout is included or billed separately ([ASEL](https://www.aselonline.com/institute/consumer-tips-resources/articles-for-consumers/estate-sale-services-what-do-they-include/)).
- **Ask how they price.** Schwab quotes ASEL's director saying good companies research what comparable items actually sold for, not internet asking prices ([Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)).
- **Ask the executor and attorney first.** In probate, selling estate property may need court authority (see Phase 2).
- **Ask a tax professional** if anything might sell for more than it was worth at the date of death. Schwab notes inherited property can trigger capital gains tax in that case ([Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)).

## Phase 6: Donating

Donating keeps usable things in use and shrinks what you pay to haul. Policies are set locally, so call first.

| Charity | What it generally takes | Pickup | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Habitat for Humanity ReStore | New and gently used furniture, appliances, housewares, building materials. Each ReStore is different | Many offer free pickup of large items. Call to confirm donation hours | [Habitat for Humanity](https://www.habitat.org/restores/donate-goods) |
| The Salvation Army | Gently used clothing, household goods, unbroken furniture, working non-built-in appliances, books, bikes, toys. Varies by location | Free pickup scheduling online or 1-800-SA-TRUCK; a fee-based faster pickup through a partner | [Salvation Army FAQ](https://www.satruck.org/Faq), [Schedule a pickup](https://satruck.org/Donate/choose) |

The Salvation Army's general **won't-take** list includes appliances that don't work or are rusty or dirty, **any gas appliances**, built-in appliances, large console TVs and stereos, TVs older than 5 years, auto parts, and paint or chemicals ([Salvation Army FAQ](https://www.satruck.org/Faq)). Habitat ReStores generally want appliances under 10 years old (see our [appliance haul-away guide](/appliance-haul-away-refrigerators-washers-dryers/)). Angi lists a typical donation pickup fee of about **$100** when a cleanout company handles it ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)).

Many junk removal companies donate as they go. 1-800-GOT-JUNK says it drops off donations at local charities ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-do/estate-cleanout)), and College HUNKS names Goodwill, Habitat ReStore, and PickUpMyDonation.com among its partners ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/junk-removal/items-we-take/estate-cleanout/)). Ask the crew for a donation receipt.

### Donation receipts and value (IRS basics)

If anyone plans to claim a deduction, the IRS rules are specific. Ask a tax professional whose return the deduction belongs on, since an estate is its own taxpayer.

- **Condition matters.** You generally can't deduct clothing or household items unless they're in **good used condition or better** ([IRS Publication 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561)).
- **Value is thrift-store value,** not what was paid. IRS says fair market value is what a willing buyer would pay, and used household items are "usually much lower than the price paid when new" ([IRS Publication 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561)).
- **Get a receipt.** For noncash gifts under $250, the IRS wants a receipt showing the charity's name and address, the date and location, and a description of the items ([IRS Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526)).
- **Over $500 means Form 8283.** Deductions over $500 for noncash gifts (or groups of similar items) need Form 8283, and over $5,000 needs a qualified appraisal ([IRS Form 8283 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283)).

A simple habit: photograph each donation load and keep the receipt with your working file.

## Phase 7: Papers, medications, and hazardous items

### Papers to keep and papers to shred

Old statements and IDs can be used for identity theft. The Iowa AG notes scammers watch obituaries and public records, and suggests securing IDs, checkbooks, tax records, and mail, and notifying banks and the three credit bureaus ([Iowa Attorney General](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/for-consumers/general-consumer-information/life-events/death-of-a-loved-one)). If you find signs of identity theft, the FTC's [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) has reporting and recovery steps.

- **Don't shred too early.** Records that support a tax return should generally be kept until the period of limitations for that return runs out, usually **3 years** and longer in some situations ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)). The executor may need them for final returns. 1-800-GOT-JUNK suggests shredding only sensitive papers that aren't needed for probate ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout)).
- **What to shred.** The Washington State Attorney General lists papers with account numbers, Social Security numbers, birth dates, signatures, and PINs, plus expired IDs and credit cards (cut them with scissors if your shredder can't take plastic). For larger jobs it suggests a commercial shredding company ([Washington State AG](https://www.atg.wa.gov/what-shred)).
- **Lots of paper?** Our sister site has guides to [shredding documents for free or cheap](https://shred.christiejustice.com/how-to-shred-documents-for-free/) and [what to shred and how long to keep records](https://shred.christiejustice.com/what-to-shred-and-how-long-to-keep-records/).

### Medications

Do this early. It's a safety issue for kids, pets, and visitors. FDA says the best option for most unused medicines is a **drug take-back location** (often a pharmacy or police station) or a **prepaid mail-back envelope**. If neither is available, check FDA's flush list, and otherwise mix pills with something unappealing like cat litter or coffee grounds, seal them in a bag, put it in the trash, and scratch personal information off the labels ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/safe-disposal-medicines/disposal-unused-medicines-what-you-should-know)).

### Paint, chemicals, batteries, and other household hazardous waste

Garages and basements in long-lived-in homes often hold old paint, cleaners, oils, batteries, and pesticides. EPA calls these household hazardous waste and says many communities have collection programs. Contact your local solid waste agency or search Earth911 for drop-off options ([U.S. EPA](https://www.epa.gov/hw/household-hazardous-waste-hhw)). Most junk removal companies and dumpster rentals won't take them. See [what junk removal companies won't take](/what-junk-removal-companies-wont-take/) for the by-hauler lists.

## Phase 9: Clean, close, and hand off

- **Declutter first, then clean.** Angi lists house cleaning at **$25 to $200 per hour**, and a deep clean as an add-on of about **$70 to $370** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)).
- **Forward the mail** to the executor, and shut off utilities once cleaning and repairs are done ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout)).
- **Do a final sweep** of every closet, attic, crawl space, and the garage before you hand over keys.

## A realistic timeline

There's no standard schedule. It depends on the estate, your state, the size of the home, and how much grief allows on a given day. These anchors come from the sources:

| When | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| First few days | Safety, arrangements, secure the home and papers. "Not every task must be completed immediately" | [Iowa Attorney General](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/for-consumers/general-consumer-information/life-events/death-of-a-loved-one) |
| Before Letters are issued | Preserve the estate only. No selling or distributing (rules vary by state) | [N.Y. EPTL § 11-1.3](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EPT/11-1.3), [1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout) |
| Within 4 months of Letters (California) | Inventory and appraisal filed with the court | [Judicial Council of California, DE-147](https://courts.ca.gov/sites/default/files/courts/default/2024-11/de147.pdf) |
| The cleanout itself | Walkthrough, dividing belongings, paperwork, and heavy lifting "can take anywhere from several days to several weeks" | [College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/blog/estate-cleanout/) |
| Haul day | A junk removal crew can usually remove a load the same visit it quotes | [1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/pricing) |

If the house has to be emptied by a closing date or a lease end, tell the estate sale company and hauler that date up front. A compressed schedule can change their quote.

## Printable checklist

1. Secure the home, mail, medications, IDs, and devices. Tell the insurer if the house is vacant.
2. Find the will and estate papers. Start one working file and log every call.
3. Stop using the person's power of attorney, cards, and accounts.
4. Talk to a local probate attorney. Confirm who has authority and what you may do before Letters.
5. Photograph every room. List heirs' requests in writing.
6. Flag possible valuables for an appraiser.
7. Sort room by room: Keep, Sell, Donate, Junk. Papers first.
8. Get medications to a take-back site.
9. Interview 2–3 estate sale companies (if selling). Get a written contract that covers unsold items.
10. Schedule donations. Keep receipts and photos.
11. Take paint and chemicals to household hazardous waste collection.
12. Shred sensitive papers the executor doesn't need, after checking record-keeping rules.
13. Get 2–3 written haul quotes, or price a dumpster plus helpers.
14. Clean, do a final sweep, forward mail, and close utilities.
15. Give the executor every receipt.

## FAQ

**How much does an estate cleanout cost?** Angi's 2026 data puts full-service estate cleanouts at **$275 to $4,000**, with a **$1,250** average and some jobs reaching **$6,000** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)). If you only need the hauling, a full junk removal truckload is **$600 to $800** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). Confirm locally.

**Can I start clearing out a parent's house right after they die?** You can usually secure it and protect what's there. Selling, giving away, or dividing property generally waits until the executor or administrator has court authority, though rules differ by state ([N.Y. EPTL § 11-1.3](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EPT/11-1.3), [1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/decluttering/estate-probate-cleanout)). Ask a local probate attorney.

**What percentage do estate sale companies take?** Commonly **35% to 40%** of gross sales ([Angi, citing ASEL](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm), [Charles Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/estate-sale-need-to-knows)), with a wider **25% to 45%** range reported by [EstateSales.org](https://estatesales.org/how-much-do-estate-sale-companies-charge). Smaller estates may face minimums.

**Will charities take everything that's left?** No. The Salvation Army, for example, won't take broken or gas appliances, built-in appliances, older TVs, or paint and chemicals ([Salvation Army](https://www.satruck.org/Faq)). Each Habitat ReStore sets its own list ([Habitat for Humanity](https://www.habitat.org/restores/donate-goods)). Call before you load the car.

**Do I need a receipt for donated items?** If anyone will claim a deduction, yes. IRS rules require a receipt with specific details, and Form 8283 for noncash deductions over $500 ([IRS Pub. 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), [Form 8283 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283)). Ask a tax professional.

**Should I rent a dumpster or hire a junk removal crew?** A dumpster is usually cheaper if family can do the loading over several days. A crew is faster and does the lifting. See [junk removal vs. dumpster rental](/junk-removal-vs-dumpster-rental-which-is-cheaper/).

**What do I do with old medications in the house?** Use a drug take-back location or mail-back envelope first, per FDA ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/safe-disposal-medicines/disposal-unused-medicines-what-you-should-know)).

**It's too much. Is it okay to hire someone for all of it?** Yes. That's what full-service estate cleanout companies are for, and many junk removal crews donate and recycle as they go ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-do/estate-cleanout), [College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/junk-removal/items-we-take/estate-cleanout/)). Take breaks, lean on your people, and set aside the things you can't decide about yet.
