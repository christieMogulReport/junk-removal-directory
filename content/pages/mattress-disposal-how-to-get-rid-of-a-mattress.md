Old mattresses are awkward. They are bulky, floppy, and hard to fit in a car, and a lot of places that take other furniture won't take them. Many cities will pick one up for free or a small fee, but a few states and cities have special rules. This guide covers every common way to get rid of a mattress or box spring, what each one costs according to published sources, and which rules to check first.

> **Quick answer.** Try the cheap options first. (1) If you're **buying a new mattress**, ask the seller to haul the old one away at delivery. It's free by law in California and a paid add-on at many retailers elsewhere. (2) Check your **city's bulky item pickup**, which is free or low cost in many places. (3) In **California, Connecticut, Oregon, or Rhode Island**, use a **Bye Bye Mattress** drop-off site, which is free for residents. (4) Otherwise, a **junk removal** pickup typically runs **$75 to $150** per mattress, about **$100 on average** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/mattress-removal-cost.htm)). In **Massachusetts**, mattresses are banned from the trash and must be recycled. In **New York City**, a mattress must be sealed in a plastic bag before it goes to the curb. Rules vary by city, so confirm with your city or hauler.

## 1. Retailer haul-away when you buy a new mattress

If a new mattress is being delivered, haul-away is usually the least work, because the crew is already at your door.

- **California requires it.** State law says a retailer must offer to pick up your used mattress **at no additional cost** when it delivers a new one. If the new mattress arrives by common carrier (for example, an online order shipped by a parcel service), the retailer must offer to arrange a pickup within **30 days** of delivery. The retailer may refuse a mattress it decides is contaminated and poses a risk to its crew or products ([Mattress Recycling Council, quoting Cal. Public Resources Code 42992](https://mattressrecyclingcouncil.org/retailer-take-back/)).
- **Elsewhere it's often a paid add-on.** For example, a Mattress Firm product page lists "Mattress Setup and Haul Away" for **$109.99**, which includes removing the old mattress set, equal to the number of pieces you buy ([Mattress Firm](https://www.mattressfirm.com/greenwood-105-firm-mattress/5637147281.p)). Mattress Firm says haul-off is included at no extra cost in California, and that parcel-shipped orders don't include haul-off ([Mattress Firm delivery FAQ](https://www.mattressfirm.com/track/track)).
- **It's usually one-for-one.** Costco's haul-away is available in select areas, on a "one-for-one, like item" basis, and the old item must be ready when the new one is delivered. Contaminated or infested items aren't eligible ([Costco](https://customerservice.costco.com/app/answers/answer_view/a_id/1018687/~/what-is-haul-away%3F)).

**Tip:** Check the checkout page for the haul-away option and its price before you pay, and make sure the number of old pieces you want removed matches what you're buying. Delivery crews can turn down a mattress that is stained, wet, or has bugs.

## 2. City or county bulky item pickup

Many cities collect mattresses as a bulky item. Rules differ a lot, so look up your own city's sanitation page. Two big-city examples:

- **Los Angeles.** LA Sanitation picks up mattresses and box springs as bulky items in an "unlimited curbside service at no additional charge." You have to schedule at least one business day before your regular trash day, online, through the MyLA311 app, or by calling 1-800-773-2489. The city also runs monthly free drop-off events ([LA Sanitation](https://sanitation.lacity.gov/services/schedule-bulky-item-pickup)).
- **New York City.** Mattresses and box springs go out with bulk trash, set at the curb between 6 PM and midnight the night before collection. They **must be sealed in a plastic bag** to prevent the spread of bed bugs, and the city doesn't supply the bags. If you don't follow the rule, the mattress may not be collected and you may be fined **$50** for a first offense, **$100** for a second, and **$200** for a third and later offenses ([NYC311](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02378), [DSNY](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/residents/collection-laws-residents.page)).

Angi notes that some areas require approved mattress bags, which it puts at about **$10 to $20** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/mattress-removal-cost.htm)).

## 3. State mattress recycling programs (CA, CT, OR, RI)

Four states have mattress recycling laws: **California, Connecticut, Oregon, and Rhode Island.** In each one, the industry-run nonprofit Mattress Recycling Council (MRC) runs a statewide program called **Bye Bye Mattress** ([MRC](https://mattressrecyclingcouncil.org/)). You already paid for it: a recycling fee is charged on every mattress and box spring sold in those states.

| State | Recycling fee per unit sold | Which mattresses drop-off sites accept |
|---|---|---|
| California | $18.00 (since April 1, 2026) | Any condition, no matter when bought. Futon mattresses count here |
| Connecticut | $19.50 (since Oct. 1, 2026) | Units in recyclable condition. Crib mattresses count here |
| Oregon | $22.50 (since Jan. 1, 2025) | Any condition, no matter when bought |
| Rhode Island | $22.50 (since Jan. 1, 2026) | Units in recyclable condition |

*Sources: fees from [MRC Registration and Reporting](https://www.mrcreporting.org/Home.aspx), the [Bye Bye Mattress FAQ](https://byebyemattress.com/faq/), and MRC's notices on the [California](https://mattressrecyclingcouncil.org/2026-california-mattress-recycling-fee-adjustment/) and [Connecticut](https://mattressrecyclingcouncil.org/new-recycling-fee-set-for-connecticut/) fee changes. Acceptance rules from the [Bye Bye Mattress FAQ](https://byebyemattress.com/faq/).*

What to know before you load the car ([Bye Bye Mattress FAQ](https://byebyemattress.com/faq/)):

- **Any resident of those four states can use the program**, even if you didn't pay the fee on your old mattress.
- The mattress must have been **used in the state** where you drop it off.
- In Connecticut and Rhode Island, mattresses that are badly damaged, heavily soiled, moldy, wet or frozen, or infested with bed bugs may not be taken free. Call the drop-off site first, because fees may apply.
- Recycled mattresses are taken apart, and the **steel, foam, fabric, and wood** are used to make new products.

Outside those four states, [Bye Bye Mattress](https://byebyemattress.com/) keeps a list of recycling facilities around the country and suggests contacting your city or waste hauler if none are nearby.

## 4. Massachusetts: mattresses are banned from the trash

Since **November 1, 2022**, Massachusetts has banned mattresses (including box springs, foam mattresses, and "mattress in a box" products) from disposal or transport for disposal. Mattresses contaminated with mold, bodily fluids, insects, oil, or hazardous substances are excluded from the ban ([MassDEP FAQ](https://www.mass.gov/doc/frequently-asked-questions-mattress-textile-waste-ban/download), [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/news/new-waste-disposal-ban-regulations-take-effect-today)). MassDEP says it generally enforces the ban against businesses, cities, and waste haulers rather than individuals. Still, it means your trash hauler and your town have to handle mattresses as recycling.

MassDEP's advice for residents is to donate mattresses in good condition, use retailer haul-away when you buy a new one, and ask your city or town about its collection program ([Mass.gov: Mattress recycling](https://www.mass.gov/guides/mattress-recycling)). The state says more than 75% of a mattress's components can be reused once it's taken apart.

## 5. Drop it off yourself (landfill or transfer station)

If you have a truck or a vehicle that fits a mattress, your county landfill or transfer station may take it for a fee. Angi puts DIY disposal at about **$40 to $80** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/mattress-removal-cost.htm)). Some places now recycle mattresses instead of burying them and charge per piece. For example, Rutherford County, Tennessee stopped landfilling mattresses on August 1, 2026, and now charges a **$25 recycling fee per mattress and $25 per box spring** at its transfer station ([Rutherford County, TN](https://rutherfordcountytn.gov/transfer_station)).

Before you go, call or check the website for hours, residency rules, the per-mattress fee, and whether it accepts mattresses at all. Strap the mattress down well. An unsecured mattress on a car roof is a road hazard.

## 7. Toss it in a dumpster you're already renting

A dumpster only makes sense for a mattress if you're already renting one for a cleanout. Even then, many dumpster companies charge **per-item surcharges** for mattresses. For example, one Georgia company's fee schedule lists a **$25 mattress surcharge** ([Dumpee Dumpsters](https://dumpeedumpsters.com/company-policies/)). Some don't allow mattresses at all. Our [won't-take guide](/what-junk-removal-companies-wont-take/) compares the rules, and our [dumpster sizes guide](/dumpster-rental-sizes-explained/) lists other per-item fees. EPA's conversion table puts a mattress at about **55 lb** ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-03/documents/conversions.pdf)), so weight is rarely the problem. The fee is.

## Can I donate my mattress?

Sometimes, but don't count on it.

- **Habitat for Humanity ReStore:** Habitat's national donation guide says "Mattresses and box springs are not accepted" ([Habitat for Humanity](https://www.habitat.org/restore/should-i-donate-habitat-restore)). A few local ReStores host recycling drop-offs instead, so check your local store.
- **Salvation Army:** Accepted items vary by location because of local rules and needs, so check with your local thrift store ([Salvation Army](https://satruck.org/faq)).
- **Condition matters.** Angi says donation centers typically reject mattresses with bed bugs or fleas, heavy stains, odors, tears, broken springs, or heavy damage ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/mattress-removal-cost.htm)).

Local shelters or community buy-nothing groups may take a clean mattress. Ask first, and be honest about its condition.

## If the mattress has bed bugs

New York City's health department advises ([NYC DOHMH](https://www.stoppests.org/stoppests/assets/File/bed-bugs-disposal.pdf)):

1. **Get the home inspected and treated first.** Most bedding and furniture from a home with bed bugs can be treated and doesn't need to be thrown out. Throwing out the bed alone won't fix an infestation.
2. **Wrap it in the room where it was used.** Use heavy-duty plastic and seal it with duct tape so bugs don't spread through the house on the way out.
3. **Label it** "BED BUG INFESTED - DO NOT TAKE," and cut or deface it so nobody takes it home.
4. **Put it out close to collection time**, and **don't donate** items from an infested home.

Tell your hauler about bed bugs up front. Retailers, Costco haul-away, and some recycling sites can refuse infested mattresses (see above), and junk removal companies may charge extra.

## Don't dump it

Leaving a mattress in an alley, a vacant lot, or a park is illegal dumping ([Bye Bye Mattress](https://byebyemattress.com/)). Be careful with anyone who offers to take one for cash and won't say where it goes. Our [scams and red flags guide](/junk-removal-and-dumpster-rental-scams-red-flags/) explains why cash-only haulers who dump illegally can leave you exposed, and how to check a hauler first.

## FAQ

**What's the cheapest way to get rid of a mattress?** Usually your city's bulky pickup (often free or about $10 to $20), a free state drop-off site in CA, CT, OR, or RI, or free retailer haul-away in California when you buy a new mattress. Angi lists donation at $0, but few charities accept mattresses.

**Can I put a mattress out with my regular trash?** It depends on your city. Many collect mattresses only on bulky item days or by appointment. NYC requires a sealed plastic bag. Massachusetts bans mattresses from the trash entirely. Check your city's sanitation page before putting it out.

**Do I have to take the box spring too?** Haulers and retailers usually count the box spring as a separate piece. Retailer haul-away often matches the number of pieces you buy ([Mattress Firm](https://www.mattressfirm.com/greenwood-105-firm-mattress/5637147281.p)), state recycling fees are charged per unit, and Angi puts box spring removal at about $20 to $40 extra.

**Why did the delivery crew refuse my old mattress?** Retailers can refuse mattresses that are contaminated or infested. California law allows this for items that pose a risk to personnel or products ([MRC](https://mattressrecyclingcouncil.org/retailer-take-back/)), and Costco excludes contaminated or infested items ([Costco](https://customerservice.costco.com/app/answers/answer_view/a_id/1018687/~/what-is-haul-away%3F)). Ask about condition rules before delivery day.
