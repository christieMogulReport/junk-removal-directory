Both options get the junk off your property. The difference is **who does the lifting** and **how you are charged**. A junk removal company sends a crew that loads everything and prices by how much space your stuff takes up in its truck. A dumpster company drops off an empty roll-off box, you fill it on your own schedule, and the price is based on box size, rental days, and weight. Which one is cheaper depends almost entirely on the project.

> **Quick answer.** For **one item up to about half a truckload** (a couch, a few appliances, a small garage clear-out), junk removal is usually cheaper or close to it, and the crew does the work. For **big cleanouts, remodels that run for days, and anything over a full truckload**, a dumpster usually costs less, because you supply the labor. **Heavy debris** like concrete, brick, dirt, and roofing is the tricky one: weight limits and overage fees decide the price for both options, so get quotes based on weight. These are rules of thumb from published national ranges, not quotes. Confirm with your hauler and your city.

| Space needed | None | Room for the box and the delivery truck, or a street permit |

**Junk removal pricing.** 1-800-GOT-JUNK prices multi-item jobs in eighths of a truck and gives a free on-site estimate. A single item is priced on its own, by size ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/pricing)). College HUNKS also prices by the space items take up in its truck, which it lists as 11 ft long, 8 ft wide and 5 ft tall, or about 440 cubic feet for a full load ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/pricing/junk-removal-pricing/)). That is about 16 cubic yards (one cubic yard is 27 cubic feet). LoadUp prices item by item online and adds one area service fee per pickup ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/pricing/)). Angi notes that you may have to pay for a **minimum of about 2 cubic yards** even if your load is smaller ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)).

### Single items

Renting a whole dumpster for one couch rarely makes sense. Angi puts the minimum charge for a single bulky item at **$60 to $150** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). Online, item-priced services can come in lower if you leave things outside. LoadUp's published sample for one Atlanta ZIP code (30312) shows a couch at $30, a queen mattress at $35, and a $59 area service fee, for a $124 guaranteed total. It takes $5 off for curbside pickup and charges extra for stairs and heavy items ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/pricing/)). 1-800-GOT-JUNK advertises online pricing for items left outside "starting at just $39" ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/pricing)). Those are examples, not your price. Local rates differ.

### Garage and basement cleanouts

This is where the two options are closest. If your pile fits in about **a quarter to half a truck**, Angi's $125–$400 range for junk removal overlaps the low end of a 10 yard dumpster ($225–$575), and the crew does the lifting. Once you are past half a truck, or you want to sort over a few weekends, a dumpster usually wins. Angi's junk removal article itself says renting a dumpster often costs less because you do the loading ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). Basements add stairs, which Angi says push junk removal labor toward the high end ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)).

### Estate cleanouts

Estates are usually big, and that favors a dumpster on price. 1-800-GOT-JUNK lists a half to full load for downsizing a whole 3 to 4 bedroom home ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/pricing)), and Angi's full truckload range is $600–$800. Two full loads at that range would be $1,200–$1,600 (our arithmetic). A 30 or 40 yard dumpster runs $300–$800 a week, but someone has to sort and carry everything out.

If you hire help to load, Angi lists professional movers at **$25 to $50 per hour** and staircase hauling at about **$40 to $100 per staircase** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)). Full-service estate cleanout companies, which also sort, donate, and sell, run **$275 to $4,000** in Angi's data, and estate sale services often take **35% to 40%** of gross proceeds ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/estate-cleanout-services-cost.htm)). Sorting out donations and items to sell first shrinks the load for either option. For the full step-by-step process (probate timing, selling, donating, shredding, and hauling), see our [estate cleanout checklist](/estate-cleanout-checklist/).

### Remodel debris

Remodels produce debris over days or weeks, which suits a box that stays on site. Angi lists extra-day fees of about **$5 to $10 per day** past the rental period ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-dumpster-rental.htm)). With junk removal you would pay for a new trip each time the pile builds up.

Watch the weight. The U.S. EPA's conversion table lists loose sheetrock (drywall) scrap at about **394 lb per cubic yard** and loose 6-inch ceramic tile at about **1,214 lb per cubic yard** ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-03/documents/conversions.pdf)). Using those numbers (our math): a 20 yard box filled with drywall scrap would be about 7,870 lb, roughly **3.9 tons**. Angi lists a 2–3 ton weight limit for a 20 yard box ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-dumpster-rental.htm)), so you could owe overage at **$40 to $200 per extra ton**. Tell the hauler what you are tossing and ask for the included tonnage in writing.

### Roofing and heavy debris

Heavy material changes the math for both options. EPA weights ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-03/documents/conversions.pdf)):

| Material | EPA weight estimate |
|---|---|
| Asphalt shingles, loose | about 419 lb per cubic yard |
| Asphalt/tar roofing | 2,919 lb per cubic yard |
| Concrete scrap, loose | 1,855 lb per cubic yard |
| Brick, whole | 3,024 lb per cubic yard |
| Soil (sandy loam), loose | 2,392 lb per cubic yard |

Our arithmetic from those figures: 10 cubic yards of loose shingles is about 4,190 lb, or about **2.1 tons**. 10 cubic yards of loose concrete is about 18,550 lb, or about **9.3 tons**. Angi lists a 2–3 ton weight limit for a 10 yard box ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-dumpster-rental.htm)). Budget Dumpster says that with brick or concrete you can typically fill the box only about halfway ([Budget Dumpster](https://www.budgetdumpster.com/pricing.php)), and WM notes that some dumpster sizes don't take concrete, bricks, or demolition material ([WM](https://www.wm.com/us/en/support/wm-services/what-materials-are-not-allowed-in-roll-off-dumpster)). Ask for a heavy-material or clean-load box.

Junk removal isn't automatically the way around this. 1-800-GOT-JUNK lists concrete among the renovation waste it takes ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-take)), and College HUNKS says it prices construction and demolition debris by volume **and weight** ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/pricing/junk-removal-pricing/)). Angi notes that in areas with high disposal rates, construction debris can cost up to about **$1,300** for a 20-yard dumpster's worth ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). For heavy debris, get a written price from both kinds of company based on the material and its estimated weight.

## Labor and time: the hidden cost of "cheaper"

A dumpster is cheaper mostly because **you are the labor**. Angi puts junk removal labor at **$100 to $300** of a typical bill, already included in the price ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). With a dumpster, add your own time or the cost of helpers. Think about:

- **Stairs, distance, and heavy items.** These raise junk removal labor ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)), and they are also what makes DIY loading hard and risky.
- **Being there.** Dumpsters.com says you usually need to be present for junk removal, and that weather can slow down a dumpster project ([Dumpsters.com](https://www.dumpsters.com/blog/dumpster-rentals-vs-junk-removal)).
- **Pace.** A dumpster lets you sort over several days. Junk removal is a single visit.

### Permit examples

Permit rules are local, so call your city or county before you put a box on the street. Two published examples:

- **Costa Mesa, California:** a $105 permit covers 14 days for a bin in the street or public right-of-way. The bin must come from a city-approved franchise hauler, and unpermitted containers can be impounded ([City of Costa Mesa](https://www.costamesaca.gov/government/departments-and-divisions/public-works/waste-collection-and-recycling/temporary-bin-rentals-public-right-of-way-row-permits)).
- **Jefferson County, Colorado:** a permit is required for a dumpster or roll-off on a public street. Each permit is $50, plus 2.5% when paying by card, with about 48 hours for review ([Jefferson County](https://www.jeffco.us/4351/Permit-for-Dumpster-Roll-Off-Container-P)).

Keeping the box on your driveway often avoids a permit. Check with your city and HOA anyway.

## What each one won't take

| | Dumpster (WM's general list) | Junk removal (1-800-GOT-JUNK's list) |
|---|---|---|
| Not accepted | Aerosol cans, all liquids, antifreeze, appliances, asbestos, batteries, chemicals, computers, monitors and televisions, dirt, fluorescent tubes, hazardous waste, medical waste, microwaves, motor oil, paint (except dried latex), propane tanks, railroad ties, solvents, tires, and more. Some sizes also exclude concrete, brick, and demolition material ([WM](https://www.wm.com/us/en/support/wm-services/what-materials-are-not-allowed-in-roll-off-dumpster)) | Chemicals, solvents and oils, asbestos, storage and oil drums, and oil and waste storage tanks ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-take)) |
| Notable items it does take | Furniture and general household junk, within the hauler's rules | Refrigerators and freezers, TVs, mattresses, hot tubs, concrete, insulation, yard waste ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-take)) |

The two lists don't match. Appliances, TVs, and tires are often banned from dumpsters but commonly taken by junk haulers, sometimes for a fee. That can tip a mixed cleanout toward junk removal, or toward a dumpster for the bulk plus a junk pickup for the leftovers. Both lists vary by location, so get your hauler's list in writing.

## Decision checklist

Go through these in order. The more answers in the right-hand column, the more a dumpster makes sense.

| Question | Leans junk removal | Leans dumpster |
|---|---|---|
| How much is there? | One item to about half a truckload | More than a full truckload |
| Can you (or helpers) carry it all? | No: heavy items, stairs, health limits | Yes |
| How long will the project take? | One day, done | Several days or weeks (remodel, sorting an estate) |
| Is there room for a box? | No driveway or space, or a street permit is costly | Driveway or yard space for the box and truck |
| Is it heavy debris? | Small amount, priced by weight | Large amount with a heavy-material box and clear tonnage |
| Is it banned in dumpsters (appliances, TVs, tires)? | Yes, a lot of it | Little or none |
| Do you need a firm price before booking? | Online item pricing (LoadUp, outdoor pickup) or an on-site quote | Flat-rate quote with tonnage, days, and fees listed |

**Before you book either one, ask:**

1. What is the **total price**, including disposal, fuel, taxes, and any minimum?
2. For dumpsters: how many **tons and days** are included, and what is the **overage rate** per ton and per day?
3. For junk removal: is the price **per item or by truck volume**, and what costs extra (stairs, heavy items, tires, paint, disassembly)?
4. Can I get the quote **in writing**, by text or email?
5. What **won't you take**, and where should I take those items?
6. For dumpsters on the street: **do I need a permit**, and who gets it?

Our [dumpster sizes guide](/dumpster-rental-sizes-explained/) has more questions to ask before renting a box.

## FAQ

**Is it cheaper to rent a dumpster or hire junk removal?** For small jobs, junk removal is usually cheaper. For large jobs, a dumpster usually is. Angi says renting a dumpster often costs less than full-service hauling because you do the loading, and that the largest 40 cubic yard roll-off holds more than a standard haul-away truck ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)).

**Can I use both?** Yes. A common approach is a dumpster for the bulk, plus a junk pickup for items the dumpster company won't take, like appliances or TVs, or for a single heavy item you can't lift. Compare the combined cost to one full-service visit.

**What about hauling it to the dump myself?** Angi puts landfill fees at about $50 to $100 per ton and says self-hauling only saves money if you have a truck and the time ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). Call the landfill to check what it accepts and its hours.

**Why did my junk removal quote change on site?** Many full-service companies price after seeing the items, because the price depends on how much truck space they fill ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/pricing), [College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/pricing/junk-removal-pricing/)). Photos and a clear list up front help you get a closer estimate. Online item-priced services set the price before booking, as long as the items match ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/pricing/)).
