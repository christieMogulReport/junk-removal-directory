Picking a dumpster size feels like a volume question, but it is really two questions: **how much space** your stuff takes up, and **how much it weighs**. Most people only plan for the first one, and that is how they end up with overweight fees. This guide covers both.

> **Quick answer.** For a garage, basement, or single-room cleanout, most people land on a **10 or 15 yard** dumpster. For a kitchen or bathroom remodel, a large cleanout, or a deck, think **20 yard**. For a whole-house renovation, estate cleanout, or roofing job, think **30 or 40 yard**. If you are between two sizes, hauler guides commonly suggest the larger one, because a second delivery usually costs more than a bigger box. And if you are throwing away concrete, brick, dirt, or roofing, **weight decides the size, not volume**.

## What "yard" means

Dumpster sizes are measured in cubic yards. One cubic yard is a box 3 feet wide, 3 feet long, and 3 feet high, or 27 cubic feet ([WM](https://www.wm.com/us/en/dumpster-rental)). A "20 yard" dumpster holds about 20 of those boxes **when filled level with the top**. Nothing should stick out above the sides. Haulers can refuse to haul an overfilled box or charge an overflow fee ([WM](https://www.wm.com/us/en/dumpster-rental), [Roll-Off Dumpster Direct](https://www.rolloffdumpsterdirect.com/faqs/)).

Dimensions and weight allowances differ by hauler and by city, so treat this table as typical, not exact. Even one national company's own pages list slightly different dimensions for the same size.

| Size | Typical dimensions (L × W × H) | Pickup-truck loads (a) | Weight included (typical range) | Typical price per week (b) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 yard | 12 × 8 × 3.5 ft | about 4 | 1–2 tons | $225–$575 |
| 15 yard | about 14–16 × 7.5–8 × 4–4.5 ft | about 6 | 2–3 tons | $350–$500 (hauler estimate) |
| 20 yard | 22 × 8 × 4 ft | about 8 | 2–4 tons | $275–$700 |
| 30 yard | 22 × 8 × 5–6 ft | about 14 | 3–5 tons | $300–$725 |
| 40 yard | 22 × 8 × 8 ft | about 16 | 4–6 tons | $350–$800 |

(a) Pickup-truck loads are from WM's roll-off size guide. Other haulers publish different numbers for the same size.

(b) Weekly price ranges for 10, 20, 30, and 40 yard are from Angi's 2026 cost data, and the 15 yard range is a hauler estimate from Haul Yeah!. Weight ranges are from Haul Yeah!'s size guide. Sources are listed at the bottom of this page.

**Real hauler examples show how much the weight allowance varies.** Eko Services lists 1.5 tons included with a 10 yard, 2 tons with a 15 yard, 2.5 tons with a 20 yard, and 4.5 tons with a 40 yard. A Springfield, Illinois hauler (Lake Area Disposal) lists 3 tons with a 10 yard, 4.5 with a 15, 6 with a 20, and 7.5 with a 30. Same box size, different allowance, so always read the quote.

## The weight trap: why a half-full dumpster can be "full"

Volume tells you what fits. Weight tells you what you will pay. Heavy debris hits the weight limit long before the box looks full. Here are weights from the U.S. EPA's volume-to-weight conversion table, so you can see the difference:

| Material | EPA weight estimate |
|---|---|
| Mixed construction and demolition debris (composite to bulk) (c) | 417 to 484 lb per cubic yard |
| Asphalt shingles, loose | about 419 lb per cubic yard |
| Sheetrock (drywall) scrap, loose | about 394 lb per cubic yard |
| Concrete scrap, loose | 1,855 lb per cubic yard |
| Soil (sandy loam), loose | 2,392 lb per cubic yard |
| Gravel, loose | 2,565 lb per cubic yard |
| Brick, whole | 3,024 lb per cubic yard |
| Refrigerator | 267 lb each |
| Washer | 177 lb each |
| Dryer | 130 lb each |
| Mattress | 55 lb each |

*Sources: all rows except the mixed construction debris row are from the [EPA Standard Volume-to-Weight Conversion Factors table](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-03/documents/conversions.pdf). (c) The mixed construction and demolition debris row is from a different EPA document, the [Volume-to-Weight Conversion Factors memorandum (April 2016)](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-04/documents/volume_to_weight_conversion_factors_memorandum_04192016_508fnl.pdf), which lists "Remainder/Composite Construction and Demolition" at 417 lb per cubic yard and "Construction & Demolition Bulk" at 484 lb per cubic yard. These are averages, and wet debris weighs more. The two EPA documents use different methods, so their figures for the same material can differ.*

**Rough arithmetic using those numbers** (our math, not a quote):

- A 10 yard box filled with loose concrete scrap would be about 10 × 1,855 = 18,550 lb, or roughly **9 tons**. A 10 yard allowance of 1 to 3 tons would be used up by a fraction of a box.
- A 20 yard box filled to the top with mixed construction debris at EPA's 417 to 484 lb per cubic yard would be about 8,340 to 9,680 lb, roughly **4.2 to 4.8 tons**. That is above the 2 to 4 ton range many haulers include.
- The same 20 yard box of furniture and household clutter will weigh far less, which is why household cleanouts are usually limited by space and remodels and demolition by weight.

**What to do about it:** Tell the hauler exactly what you are throwing away. For concrete, brick, dirt, or roofing, ask for a "heavy material" or "clean load" dumpster. Haulers commonly sell small boxes, often 10 yard or smaller, for this, and may require the material to be kept separate from other trash. Do not mix it into a general debris box.

Extra weight is billed per ton or per pound. Angi puts typical overweight charges at **$40 to $200 per extra ton** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-dumpster-rental.htm)). Published examples include $65 per ton (Dumpee Dumpsters), $75 per ton (Lake Area Disposal), and $0.03 per pound, which is about $60 per ton (Eko Services).

## How much does a dumpster rental cost?

Angi's 2026 data puts the national average rental at about **$386**, with most homeowners paying **$294 to $480** for a typical rental, and extremes from about $200 up to about $800 ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-dumpster-rental.htm)). Price depends on size, weight, debris type, rental length, and where you live. Angi's examples of city ranges run from roughly $300 to $750 in Columbus, OH to $500 to $1,000 in New York City. For a full breakdown of prices by size, rental period, and city, plus every common add-on fee, see [How much does a dumpster rental cost?](/how-much-does-a-dumpster-rental-cost/)

Common add-ons to look for in the quote:

- **Permit** to place the dumpster on a street or sidewalk: roughly $10 to $100, depending on the city ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-dumpster-rental.htm)). Driveway placement often needs none, but check.
- **Extra days:** most rentals include about 7 days. Published examples run from $10 a day (Lake Area Disposal) to $15 (Eko) to $20 (Dumpee).
- **Per-item fees** for mattresses, tires, and appliances with refrigerant. Examples: mattress $25 to $30, tire $15 to $20, refrigerant appliance $40 to $65 ([Dumpee](https://dumpeedumpsters.com/company-policies/), [Chuck-It Haulers](https://www.chuckithaulers.com/roll-offs)).
- **Contamination, wasted-trip, and relocation fees:** Dumpee lists $150 to $350 for contamination, $125 for a failed pickup, and $75 to $150 to relocate. Roll-Off Dumpster Direct says a wasted-trip fee of up to $500 is typical if you are not ready when the driver arrives.

## Dumpster vs. junk removal: which is cheaper?

If you only have a few items, a dumpster is usually overkill. Angi's 2026 data puts the national average for full-service junk removal at **$241**, with most people paying **$133 to $372**. It prices by how much of a 20-yard truck you fill ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)):

| Amount (20-yard truck) | Angi average |
|---|---|
| ⅛ truckload (2–3 cubic yards) | $75–$125 |
| ¼ truckload (3–4.5 cubic yards) | $125–$200 |
| ½ truckload (4.5–11 cubic yards) | $200–$400 |
| ¾ truckload (11–13 cubic yards) | $400–$600 |
| Full truckload (13–17 cubic yards) | $600–$800 |

Compare that with a dumpster, where Angi's weekly ranges are $225 to $575 for 10 yards and $275 to $700 for 20 yards. Rule of thumb from these numbers (our comparison of two Angi articles, not a quote): **below about half a truckload, junk removal is competitive and saves you the loading. For a full truckload or more, or any project that runs for several days, a dumpster tends to cost less per cubic yard, because you supply the labor.** Angi's own junk removal article notes that renting a dumpster often costs less than full-service hauling since you do the loading. Junk removal wins when you need heavy lifting, have stairs, are short on time, or have no room for a box.

## Checklist: choose your size in 5 steps

1. **List what is going.** Furniture and clutter, or debris from demolition, roofing, concrete, or dirt?
2. **Estimate volume in pickup-truck loads.** Use the chart above. If you are unsure, compare piles to a pickup bed.
3. **Flag the heavy stuff.** Anything on the weight table above that is heavy per cubic yard (concrete, brick, soil, gravel) means asking about weight allowance, or a heavy-material box.
4. **Measure your space.** Allow the box length (about 12 feet for a 10 yard, about 22 feet for 20 to 40 yard) plus room for the truck. WM asks for a level surface, no low wires or branches, and a long straight approach for drop-off.
5. **Add a cushion.** If you are between sizes, ask the hauler about the next size up, and compare the small price difference to the cost of a second delivery.

## What to ask a hauler before booking

- What is the **total price** with delivery, pickup, taxes, and fuel surcharge?
- How many **days** are included, and what does each extra day cost?
- How many **tons are included**, and what is the **overage rate** per ton?
- Is there a **fill line**, and what happens if debris is above it?
- What **can't go in** the box? Ask specifically about mattresses, appliances, tires, electronics, paint, and yard waste. Many haulers prohibit some of these and charge fees for others.
- Do I need a **permit**, and do you handle it? What if the box goes on the street?
- Will the truck **damage my driveway**, and what do you need for access (width, height, wires)?
- What are your fees for **cancellation, missed pickup, relocation, and a not-ready trip**?
- Can I get a **written quote** by email or text, with these terms listed?
- Do you offer a **heavy material** box for concrete, brick, dirt, or roofing, and is it priced separately?

## What can't go in a dumpster

Items commonly banned from roll-offs include batteries, paint and other liquids, tires, appliances with refrigerant, televisions and computer monitors, propane tanks, hazardous waste, asbestos, and medical waste ([WM](https://www.wm.com/us/en/support/wm-services/what-materials-are-not-allowed-in-roll-off-dumpster)). Rules differ by hauler and location, so get your hauler's list in writing. If a banned item is found in your box, the hauler may leave the box until you remove it, and may charge a daily fee.

## FAQ

**What is the most popular dumpster size?** Different sources name different sizes. Tennessee Contractor Guide calls the 20 yard the most popular, and WM calls its 30 yard its most common. Both fit household cleanouts and remodels, so the answer depends on the market.

**How many pickup truck loads fit in a 10 yard dumpster?** WM's size guide says about 4. Other sources give about 3 to 4 or about 5. Plan on 3 to 5.

**What if my debris doesn't fill the box?** You usually pay for the container and the weight hauled. Some haulers charge by weight, others bundle an allowance. Ask whether you pay for the unused allowance.

**Do I need a permit?** If the dumpster stays on your own driveway or property, often not, but if it goes on a street or sidewalk you may need one. Angi puts permit cost at roughly $10 to $100. Check with your city or county, and some homeowners associations have rules too.

**Can I put a hot tub, sofa, or mattress in a dumpster?** Furniture usually yes. Mattresses often carry a per-item fee, and a few places ban them. Hot tubs are bulky and heavy, so ask first. For mattresses, see our [mattress disposal guide](/mattress-disposal-how-to-get-rid-of-a-mattress/).

**Can I overfill or load heavy stuff on one side?** No. Load evenly, keep debris below the top edge, and put the heaviest items on the bottom and spread out.

**How long can I keep it?** Rentals typically include about 7 days, and extra days are billed. Some haulers rent for a day or two for less, per Angi.
