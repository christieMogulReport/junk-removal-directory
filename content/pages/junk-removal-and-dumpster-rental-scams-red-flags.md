Most junk haulers and dumpster companies are ordinary local businesses. But two problems keep showing up in news reports and complaint sites. One is a **fake dumpster ad** on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist. You pay a stranger, a real dumpster still shows up, and weeks later someone asks you to pay again. The other is a **cheap, cash-only hauler** who dumps your junk on the side of a road, where it can be traced back to you. This guide explains how each scam works, the red flags, and what to check before you hand over money.

> **Quick answer.** Book directly with the company whose name will be on your receipt, using a phone number or website you looked up yourself, not the one in an ad or a chat. **Don't pay for a dumpster or a haul through Zelle, Venmo, or Cash App** to someone you met on a marketplace. Those payments work like cash and are hard to get back. Be wary of a price far below every other quote. Get the total price, the rental days, the weight allowance, and the extra fees **in writing** before anything is delivered or loaded. Ask a junk hauler **where your junk is going**. Confirm the details with your city and the company. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Scam 1: The fake dumpster ad ("triangulation" scam)

This is the scam getting the most attention in 2026. It works like this, as described by a dumpster broker, a TV station, the Better Business Bureau, and several dumpster companies ([Dumpster Enterprises](https://dumpsterenterprises.com/dumpster-rental-prices/fraud/), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cybersecurity/fraud-prevention/2026/scammers-use-stolen-cards-to-fulfill-fake-dumpster-rental-ads/), [KENS 5](https://www.kens5.com/article/news/crime/scam-dumpster-front-yard-san-antonio/273-70a31e4c-7737-4ba1-963e-ed18cf013fd9), [Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)):

1. A scammer posts a cheap dumpster rental on Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, Nextdoor, or another neighborhood app. The photos are often copied from a real company.
2. You answer the ad, and the "seller" asks you to pay through **Zelle, Venmo, or Cash App**.
3. The scammer books a real dumpster from a real local company, **paying with a stolen credit card**.
4. The dumpster arrives, so everything looks legitimate.
5. Days or weeks later, the real cardholder disputes the charge. The dumpster company loses the payment, and you are left with a dumpster nobody has paid for. The ad and the seller are gone.

Remackel Roll Off says the customer then has to pay the real company a second time to get the dumpster removed ([Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)). In the KENS 5 report from San Antonio, a dumpster company manager said one woman was out more than $1,500 in eight days ([KENS 5](https://www.kens5.com/article/news/crime/scam-dumpster-front-yard-san-antonio/273-70a31e4c-7737-4ba1-963e-ed18cf013fd9)).

**How common is it?** Published numbers are scarce. One rental broker, Dumpster Rental Enterprises, publishes its own data. It flagged **15** stolen-card orders in 2024, **119** in 2025, and **312** in 2026 through October 15, which it says is about **1 in every 15** of its orders this year ([Dumpster Enterprises](https://dumpsterenterprises.com/dumpster-rental-prices/fraud/), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cybersecurity/fraud-prevention/2026/scammers-use-stolen-cards-to-fulfill-fake-dumpster-rental-ads/)). That is one company's data, not a national figure. The broker says it could not find published figures on how often this happens across the industry.

**The newer twist: they book in your name.** Coastline Disposal, a Massachusetts dumpster company, warns that scammers now collect your real name, email, phone, and address in the chat, then book a real company's online order form as you. So the real company may text you a confirmation or call to verify the drop-off. Coastline's point: a call or text from a real company does not prove the person in the chat works for that company ([Coastline Disposal](https://www.coastlinedisposal.com/blog/dumpster-rental-scam-how-to-protect-yourself)).

**Other versions of the same ad scam:**

- **Deposit and disappear.** You pay a deposit and the dumpster never comes. One person told the BBB Scam Tracker they paid a $250 deposit on a $390 rental, the dumpster never arrived, and the seller stopped answering ([BBB Scam Tracker report](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker/lookupscam/1402965)).
- **Repeat orders in your name.** A Florida person told the BBB Scam Tracker they paid a Facebook seller $500 by Zelle twice. They later received bills from a large waste company for dumpsters ordered in their name, saying they owed $3,200 ([BBB Scam Tracker report](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker/lookupscam/953652)).
- **The "pay me again" visit.** A Washington person reported paying $400 through a cash app, then paying a man who showed up and demanded $800 or he would empty the dumpster ([BBB Scam Tracker report](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker/lookupscam/1296870)).

*BBB Scam Tracker reports are accounts submitted by consumers. The BBB notes that scammers often use real business names and phone numbers. We summarize these reports as examples, not verified findings.*

## Scam 2: The cash-only hauler who dumps your junk

The second problem is on the junk removal side. Someone offers to haul everything away for cash at a low price, then dumps it in a ditch, a vacant lot, or a back road. That can come back on you.

An environmental enforcement page from **Ector County, Texas** puts it plainly: "If you hire someone to haul away your trash or materials and they dump them illegally, you may be held liable and charges filed against you." The county also warns that many for-hire companies advertising junk hauling "may not be properly permitted," and advises you to ask the company where it plans to recycle or dispose of the materials ([Ector County, TX](https://www.ectorcountytx.gov/273/Environmental-Enforcement)). Under the Texas law the county quotes, it is an offense to dispose of "or permit the disposal of" solid waste at a place that is not an approved site ([Ector County, TX](https://www.ectorcountytx.gov/273/Environmental-Enforcement)). Rules and penalties differ by state and city, so check with your local code enforcement or solid waste office.

**Red flags for a junk hauler:**

- **Cash only, no receipt, no business name.** Washington's contractor agency lists "only accepts cash" and payment in a personal name as warning signs ([Washington L&I](https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/hiring-a-contractor/hire-smart-step-by-step)).
- **Can't or won't say where the junk is going.** Ask, as Ector County advises ([Ector County, TX](https://www.ectorcountytx.gov/273/Environmental-Enforcement)).
- **A price far below everyone else.** Washington L&I lists an offer that sounds "too good to be true" as a warning sign ([Washington L&I](https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/hiring-a-contractor/hire-smart-step-by-step)). Landfills charge by weight, so somebody has to pay to get rid of your junk legally ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cybersecurity/fraud-prevention/2026/scammers-use-stolen-cards-to-fulfill-fake-dumpster-rental-ads/)).
- **No written price before loading.** Established companies tell you the price up front. 1-800-GOT-JUNK says its crews give an all-inclusive, no-obligation price on site before they start, and you can say no ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/pricing)). LoadUp gives a guaranteed price online before booking ([LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/pricing/)).
- **Credentials you can't verify.** Ask for the legal business name and any license or permit your city requires, then check it. Washington L&I lists credentials that "can't be verified" as a scam warning sign ([Washington L&I](https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/hiring-a-contractor/hire-smart-step-by-step)).
- **No insurance.** Remackel warns that unlicensed, uninsured operators may leave you liable for property damage or injuries on your property ([Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)).

## Scam 3: Bait-and-switch prices and surprise fees

Not every bad experience is fraud. Some are just a low price on the phone and a much higher bill later. Remackel Roll Off lists these as common dumpster problems ([Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)):

- **Bait and switch.** A low quote on the phone, then hidden fees after delivery, such as administrative, fuel, or disposal charges.
- **Weight overcharges.** Billing you for more weight than you actually threw away, which inflates overage fees.
- **Early pickup.** Picking up the dumpster before your rental period ends, then charging you for another one to finish the job.

A related complaint from the BBB Scam Tracker: someone booked a dumpster online for $636.90 plus a $300 "refundable" deposit. Three hours later, they were told to pay $650 more before the order could go ahead. They said the rental charge was refunded, but not the deposit ([BBB Scam Tracker report](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker/lookupscam/1295069)).

**How to protect yourself:**

- **Get everything in writing.** Ask for a written agreement with the rental period, delivery and pickup dates, and every possible fee, including overage ([Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)).
- **Ask how weight is measured, and ask for the scale ticket.** Dumpster weight is usually measured on a scale at the landfill or transfer station. Some companies give you the ticket. For example, DumpsterX says it prorates overage from the landfill's certified scale ticket, "which you receive" ([DumpsterX](https://dumpsterxrentals.com/blog/how-dumpster-rental-pricing-works/)). If a company won't show you the weight slip, that tells you something. For how weight limits and overage work, see our [dumpster sizes guide](/dumpster-rental-sizes-explained/).
- **Refuse the wrong box.** If the dumpster that shows up isn't the size you ordered, refuse it and call the company directly ([Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)).
- **Compare two or three real quotes.** Our guide to [junk removal vs. dumpster rental](/junk-removal-vs-dumpster-rental-which-is-cheaper/) lists published national price ranges, so you can spot a quote that is way off in either direction.
- **Know what's banned.** Many surprise fees come from prohibited items like tires, paint, and mattresses. See [what junk removal companies won't take](/what-junk-removal-companies-wont-take/).

## Before you pay: a 7-point check

1. **Look the company up yourself.** Search the business name plus your town, and find a business listing with a real review history, photos, and a phone number that matches the website ([Coastline Disposal](https://www.coastlinedisposal.com/blog/dumpster-rental-scam-how-to-protect-yourself)). Check the [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/) and the [BBB Scam Tracker](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker) too.
2. **Call the number on the company's own website or listing,** not the one in the ad, a text, or a chat ([Coastline Disposal](https://www.coastlinedisposal.com/blog/dumpster-rental-scam-how-to-protect-yourself)).
3. **Book directly** with the company whose name will be on your receipt ([Dumpster Enterprises](https://dumpsterenterprises.com/dumpster-rental-prices/fraud/)).
4. **Pay with a card or the company's booking portal,** not a payment app, gift card, or wire to a personal name ([Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)). Chase says Zelle "is not intended for purchases of goods from" online marketplaces or social media, and that it most likely can't get your money back if you don't get what you paid for ([Chase](https://www.chase.com/digital/resources/privacy-security/security/use-zelle-safely)).
5. **Get a written confirmation from the company itself** with its legal business name, the price, rental days, weight allowance, and extra fees ([Dumpster Enterprises](https://dumpsterenterprises.com/dumpster-rental-prices/fraud/), [Coastline Disposal](https://www.coastlinedisposal.com/blog/dumpster-rental-scam-how-to-protect-yourself)).
6. **For a junk hauler, get the price before loading** and ask where the junk is going ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/pricing), [Ector County, TX](https://www.ectorcountytx.gov/273/Environmental-Enforcement)).
7. **Slow down.** A real company can wait while you check ([Washington L&I](https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/hiring-a-contractor/hire-smart-step-by-step)).

## If it already happened

- **A dumpster is already in your driveway and you paid someone in a chat.** Call the company whose name is on the dumpster, using the number on its own website or business listing, not the number in the ad or a text. Tell them you paid a third party ([Coastline Disposal](https://www.coastlinedisposal.com/blog/dumpster-rental-scam-how-to-protect-yourself)). Don't dump the load in the street. Stop sending money to the seller.
- **You paid through an app or bank transfer.** Contact your bank and the payment app right away and report the transaction ([Chase](https://www.chase.com/digital/resources/privacy-security/security/use-zelle-safely), [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2020/02/making-mobile-payments-protect-yourself-scams)).
- **You paid by credit or debit card.** Call the card issuer and ask about disputing the charge.
- **Report it.** File a report with the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). The BBB in San Antonio told KENS 5 that victims should call police and file a report with the BBB ([KENS 5](https://www.kens5.com/article/news/crime/scam-dumpster-front-yard-san-antonio/273-70a31e4c-7737-4ba1-963e-ed18cf013fd9)). You can also report it to the [BBB Scam Tracker](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker) and to the marketplace that ran the ad.
- **A hauler dumped your junk.** Contact your local code enforcement or solid waste office. Keep any receipt, texts, photos of the truck, and the license plate, since those help show who you hired ([Ector County, TX](https://www.ectorcountytx.gov/273/Environmental-Enforcement)).

Whether you owe anything to the real dumpster company after a scam depends on the facts and your local law. If a lot of money is involved, talk to your state attorney general's consumer protection office or a lawyer.

## FAQ

**Why does a real dumpster show up if it's a scam?** Because the scammer pays a real local company with a stolen credit card. The truck and the dumpster are real. The person who took your money is not that company ([Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/), [Coastline Disposal](https://www.coastlinedisposal.com/blog/dumpster-rental-scam-how-to-protect-yourself)).

**Is it ever OK to pay a dumpster company with Venmo or Zelle?** Some small local businesses do take payment apps. But if you found the seller through a marketplace ad and they want a payment app instead of a card, treat it as a red flag. Payment apps have little or no purchase protection ([Chase](https://www.chase.com/digital/resources/privacy-security/security/use-zelle-safely), [Remackel Roll Off](https://remackelrolloff.com/scam-alert/)).

**I got a confirmation text from a company I didn't book with. What now?** Coastline says that can mean someone used your name and contact details to book a real company with a stolen card. Call that company on the number from its own website or listing, tell them you didn't place the order, and stop paying the person who sent you the ad ([Coastline Disposal](https://www.coastlinedisposal.com/blog/dumpster-rental-scam-how-to-protect-yourself)).

**Can I get in trouble if a hauler I hired dumps my stuff?** In some places, yes. Ector County, Texas, says you "may be held liable" if someone you hire dumps your trash illegally ([Ector County, TX](https://www.ectorcountytx.gov/273/Environmental-Enforcement)). Check your local rules and keep records of who you hired.
