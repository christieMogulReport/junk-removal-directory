Junk removal crews take almost anything you can carry out of a house, such as furniture, appliances, mattresses, yard waste, and renovation debris. Roll-off dumpsters take most household junk and construction debris too. Both draw the line at **hazardous materials**, and dumpster companies ban a longer list of items than junk haulers do. This guide pulls the "won't take" lists from the companies' own public pages and points you to where each item can go instead.

> **Quick answer.** Every major junk hauler we checked refuses **hazardous materials**: chemicals, solvents, oils, asbestos, and medical or biological waste. Some haul **paint, tires, and empty propane tanks**, but with conditions or extra fees. Dumpster companies are stricter. Budget Dumpster, Dumpsters.com, and WM all ban **tires, wet paint, propane tanks, fuels and oils, car and lithium batteries, hazardous waste, and medical waste**. Most also restrict **refrigerators, electronics, and mattresses**. Banned items usually go to your local **household hazardous waste (HHW) program**, a retailer take-back program, or a specialist like an asbestos contractor. Lists vary by location, so confirm with your hauler and your city before you book.

## What dumpster rental companies prohibit

Dumpster lists are longer, because everything in the box goes to a landfill or transfer station that has its own rules.

| Item | [Budget Dumpster](https://www.budgetdumpster.com/resources/what-can-you-throw-dumpster.php) | [Dumpsters.com](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/prohibited-items) | [WM](https://www.wm.com/us/en/support/wm-services/what-materials-are-not-allowed-in-roll-off-dumpster) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hazardous waste, chemicals, solvents | Not allowed | Never allowed | Not allowed |
| Paint | Wet paint, thinners, lacquers, and stains banned; dry latex paint wrapped in plastic is OK in most areas | Wet paint banned; dry latex in a bagged, sealed can OK in most areas | Banned, except dried latex paint cans (no liquids) |
| Propane tanks, fuels, oils | Not allowed | Never allowed | Propane tanks, motor oil, and other flammable liquids not allowed |
| Tires | Not allowed | Never allowed | Not allowed |
| Car batteries / lithium-ion batteries | Not allowed (alkaline AA/AAA/D are OK) | Never allowed | Batteries not allowed |
| Refrigerators / Freon appliances | Not allowed, unless drained by an HVAC professional in some areas | Not allowed unless a professional removes the Freon | Appliances not allowed |
| Water heaters / hot water tanks | Not allowed | Never allowed | Not listed |
| Electronics (TVs, computers, monitors) | Sometimes, with a per-item charge where e-waste recycling is mandated; banned in some locations | Look for a dedicated e-waste facility | Computers, monitors, TVs, and microwaves not allowed |
| Mattresses | Only where allowed, possibly with a per-mattress fee | Only in some locations, possibly with extra fees | Not allowed for Massachusetts customers |
| Asbestos | Not allowed; hire a licensed abatement contractor | Not named separately (hazardous waste is never allowed) | Not allowed |
| Medical / infectious waste | Not allowed; use a licensed medical waste hauler | Never allowed | Not allowed |
| Railroad ties / treated wood | Not allowed | Never allowed (also telephone poles) | Railroad ties not allowed |
| Fluorescent tubes | Not listed | Not allowed (mercury) | Not allowed |

WM says its list varies by location and dumpster size, and that larger 30+ yard containers may also prohibit concrete, brick, and demolition material ([WM](https://www.wm.com/us/en/support/wm-services/what-materials-are-not-allowed-in-roll-off-dumpster)). Budget Dumpster also warns that local rules can block items it normally accepts ([Budget Dumpster](https://www.budgetdumpster.com/resources/what-can-you-throw-dumpster.php)).

**What it costs if you toss a banned item.** Dumpsters.com says the driver may refuse to pick up the box until the item is removed, which means a failed trip fee. If a banned item turns up at the landfill, you get a prohibited item fee ([Dumpsters.com](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/prohibited-items)). Dumpsters.com puts the fee for some restricted items at **$25 to $200 per item**, and says you could also face fines ([Dumpsters.com: How to load a dumpster](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/how-to-load-a-dumpster)). Budget Dumpster's commercial terms say the customer stays liable for prohibited materials, including fines and penalties ([Budget Dumpster terms](https://www.budgetdumpster.com/rental-terms-and-conditions/commercial-dumpster.php)).

## Where to take it instead

The EPA says many communities run household hazardous waste collection programs. It suggests checking with your local environmental, health, or solid waste agency, or searching "household hazardous waste" by ZIP code on Earth911. If your area has no year-round site, look for a one-day collection event. If there is neither, some local businesses take certain products, such as garages that accept used motor oil ([U.S. EPA: Household Hazardous Waste](https://www.epa.gov/hw/household-hazardous-waste-hhw)).

### Retailer example: Best Buy electronics recycling

Best Buy's recycling page says most stores accept up to ten items per household per day for recycling, no matter where you bought them. Many items are free. Some TVs cost **$29.99 per item** in store, with no drop-off fee in California, Connecticut, and Hawaii. Best Buy also offers TV haul-away for **$49.99** when a replacement is delivered, and a **$199.99** standalone haul-away for up to two large products. ## Before you book: questions to ask

1. **What's on your won't-take list for my address?** Dumpsters.com includes your area's prohibited items in the service agreement ([Dumpsters.com](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/prohibited-items)). For a junk hauler, ask the local office.
2. **Which items cost extra** (tires, paint, mattresses, appliances, electronics), and how much each?
3. **Does the item need to be prepped?** Examples: dried paint, an empty and disconnected propane tank, a drained refrigerator.
4. **What happens if a banned item is found?** Ask about trip fees and per-item charges.
5. **Where does my city take HHW,** and when is the next collection day?

## FAQ

**Will a junk removal company take paint?** Some will. College HUNKS takes prepared paint and may charge extra disposal fees, and LoadUp takes oil- and latex-based paint. 1-800-GOT-JUNK's list doesn't mention paint, but it refuses chemicals, solvents, and oils. Oil-based paint is household hazardous waste ([College HUNKS](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/junk-removal/items-we-take/paint-disposal/), [LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/waste-removal/), [1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-take)).

**Can I put a propane tank in a dumpster?** No. Budget Dumpster, Dumpsters.com, and WM all prohibit propane tanks. Contact a propane company, your fire department, or an HHW center ([Dumpsters.com](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/prohibited-items)).

**Why will a junk hauler take my refrigerator when a dumpster can't?** Refrigerants are the issue. Dumpster companies ban Freon appliances unless a professional removes the refrigerant ([Dumpsters.com](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/prohibited-items)). Some junk haulers, like 1-800-GOT-JUNK and Junk King, list refrigerators among the items they take ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/what-we-take), [Junk King](https://www.junk-king.com/what-we-take)). For every way to get rid of an old fridge, washer, or dryer, including utility rebates, city Freon pickup, and retailer haul-away, see [Appliance haul-away](/appliance-haul-away-refrigerators-washers-dryers/).

**Who removes asbestos?** Not junk haulers or dumpster companies. The EPA recommends a trained, accredited asbestos professional for removal and major repairs ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos/protect-your-family-exposures-asbestos)).
