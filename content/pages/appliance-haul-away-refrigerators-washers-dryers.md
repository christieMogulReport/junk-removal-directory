Old appliances are heavy, and the ones that keep things cold come with extra rules. Refrigerators, freezers, window air conditioners, and dehumidifiers hold refrigerant (often called "Freon"), and federal rules say it has to be recovered before the unit is scrapped or landfilled. Washers, dryers, stoves, and dishwashers are simpler: they're mostly metal, and many cities and scrap yards take them. This guide covers every common way to get rid of a major appliance, what each one costs according to published sources, and which rules to check first.

> **Quick answer.** (1) **Buying a replacement?** Add haul-away at checkout. Best Buy charges **$49.99** per appliance when it delivers the new one ([Best Buy](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/recycling/tv-appliances-haul-away/pcmcat174900050009.c?id=pcmcat174900050009)), and Lowe's lists haul-away as an extra purchase ([Lowe's](https://www.lowes.com/l/help/appliance-delivery-haulaway)). (2) **Old fridge or freezer that still works?** Check your electric utility. Many pick it up free and pay you, often **$50** ([Consumers Energy](https://www.consumersenergy.com/residential/savings-and-clean-energy/rebates/appliance-recycling), [Alliant Energy](https://www.alliantenergy.com/ways-to-save/appliance-recycling), [MidAmerican Energy](https://www.midamericanenergy.com/appliance-recycling)). (3) **Check your city's bulky pickup.** Some cities collect appliances, but fridges often need a Freon appointment, sticker, or certificate first. (4) A **junk removal** crew typically charges **$50 to $150** for a refrigerator and **$100 to $250** for a washer or dryer ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/appliance-removal-cost.htm)). (5) Don't put a fridge in a **dumpster** unless your hauler says yes in writing. Most won't take Freon appliances ([Dumpsters.com](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/prohibited-items)). Prices and rules vary, so confirm with your retailer, city, or hauler.

## First: the refrigerant rules in plain English

The rules come from Section 608 of the Clean Air Act (EPA regulations at 40 CFR Part 82, Subpart F). For household refrigerators, freezers, and window air conditioners, which usually enter the waste stream with the refrigerant still inside ([U.S. EPA: Safe disposal requirements](https://www.epa.gov/section608/stationary-refrigeration-safe-disposal-requirements)):

- **The last stop is responsible.** The final person in the disposal chain, such as a **scrap metal recycler or landfill**, must make sure the refrigerant is recovered before final disposal.
- **"Already drained" needs paperwork.** If an appliance arrives with no refrigerant left, that final processor must keep a **signed statement** with the name and address of the person who recovered the refrigerant and the date it was done.
- **A sticker alone doesn't count federally.** EPA "does not mandate or accept a sticker" as proof. Your **city** may still require its own sticker or tag for curbside pickup (examples below). That's a local rule, separate from the federal one.
- **Don't cut the lines yourself.** Angi's advice is to leave the compressor and metal tubing to professionals, because they likely contain refrigerant ([Angi: How to dispose of a refrigerator](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-dispose-of-refrigerator.htm)).

EPA's consumer page also points out that older appliances may contain **mercury switches, contaminated oil, and PCBs**, which must be removed before disposal. Some newer refrigerators use **R-290 (propane)** as the refrigerant ([U.S. EPA: Appliance disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal)). New York City says it **cannot** collect appliances with R600a or R32 refrigerant. Look for a yellow triangle warning sticker with black flames, and contact the manufacturer or a private carter instead ([DSNY: CFC removal](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/cfc-removal.page)).

**What about EPA's RAD program?** Some guides still tell you to look up a partner in EPA's Responsible Appliance Disposal (RAD) program. EPA now describes RAD as a program that ran from **2006 to 2025**. During that time partners processed over 9 million refrigerated appliances ([U.S. EPA: Appliance disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal)). Today EPA's advice for consumers is to check your **electric utility** for a rebate or turn-in program first, then your **city**, then a **retailer**. EPA also warns that appliances left at the curb without a scheduled pickup "might be landfilled or otherwise disposed of in an unsafe manner."

## 3. City or county bulky pickup (and Freon tags)

Many cities collect appliances, but refrigerant appliances usually need an extra step. A few real examples show the range:

- **New York City (free, appointment required).** Washers, dryers, ovens, dishwashers, and water heaters follow the bulk item rules: items that are mostly metal go out with recycling, up to 6 large items per collection day ([DSNY: Appliances](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/appliances.page)). For fridges, freezers, air conditioners, and water coolers, you **must** book a free CFC recovery appointment through 311. Remove hinges and locks, and set the appliance at the curb, back facing the street, after 6 PM the night before. DSNY staff remove the refrigerant and attach a tag with a six-digit number. Only tagged appliances are collected, on your next recycling day. The service is for residents only ([DSNY: CFC removal](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/cfc-removal.page)).
- **Phoenix (fees on your city bill).** Scheduled curbside pickup costs **$30** for up to five non-refrigerant appliances (washers, dryers, stoves, dishwashers, water heaters) and **$25 per refrigerant appliance** (fridges, freezers, ACs). Fridge and freezer doors, hinges, lids, glass shelves, and latches must be removed. At the transfer station, residents can drop off **one** refrigerant appliance per year free. After that it's a **$20** environmental fee plus $55 per ton (minimum $40), unless you bring a certificate showing the Freon was properly removed ([City of Phoenix: Appliance recycling](https://www.phoenix.gov/administration/departments/publicworks/residential-trash-recycling/residential-recycling/appliance-recycling.html)).
- **Darien, Connecticut (Freon sticker).** Refrigerators, dehumidifiers, and air conditioners need a **$15** Freon sticker for bulk pickup, bought at Town Hall or the transfer station scale house. Remove the fridge doors and put the sticker on the unit ([Town of Darien](https://darienct.gov/1365/Bulk-Pick-up-Appliance-Fee)).
- **Kaufman, Texas (certificate).** Weekly bulk pickup takes all appliances, but refrigerators, freezers, and air conditioners must have a **certificate verifying all Freon has been removed** ([City of Kaufman](https://www.kaufmantx.org/2256/Bulk-Pick-ups)).

**If your city wants a certificate**, EPA says you'd need a professional, such as someone from an appliance repair shop, to remove the refrigerant first ([U.S. EPA](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal)). Ask that person for a written receipt with their name, address, and the date. That matches what EPA requires final processors to keep ([U.S. EPA](https://www.epa.gov/section608/stationary-refrigeration-safe-disposal-requirements)).

**Safety:** Remove or secure fridge and freezer doors before the unit sits outside. Phoenix cites Arizona law on this, and NYC asks for hinges and locks off "to protect children and animals" ([Phoenix](https://www.phoenix.gov/administration/departments/publicworks/residential-trash-recycling/residential-recycling/appliance-recycling.html), [DSNY](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/cfc-removal.page)).

## 4. Scrap yards: washers, dryers, and stoves can pay (a little)

Washers, dryers, stoves, and water heaters are mostly steel, which scrap yards usually grade as "light iron." Fridges are trickier. Real yard policies:

- **Sahd Metal Recycling (Lancaster, PA)** posted **$0.065 per pound** for light iron on November 25, 2026. Its prohibited list includes "any scrap containing refrigerants," including freezers and refrigerators. It accepts air conditioners for a **$20** fee ([Sahd Metal Recycling](https://recycleyourmetal.com/sell-metal/)).
- **Delaware Valley Scrap (Levittown, PA)** lists washers, dryers, and refrigerators among what it buys, posts light iron at **$0.08 per pound** (effective November 24, 2026), and recovers refrigerant from AC units, refrigerators, and other appliances for **$10 a unit** ([Delaware Valley Scrap](https://www.delawarevalleyscrap.com/index.html)).
- **East Coast Metals** lists washing machines, stoves, and water heaters as light iron, and a refrigerator only with "Freon removed" ([East Coast Metals](https://ecmetals.com/services.html)).

**What's it worth?** EPA's conversion table puts the average weight at about **177 lb for a washer, 130 lb for a dryer, 181 lb for a range, 267 lb for a refrigerator, and 92 lb for a dishwasher** ([U.S. EPA: Volume-to-weight conversion factors](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-03/documents/conversions.pdf)). At the two posted light-iron prices above ($0.065 to $0.08 per pound), a 177 lb washer works out to roughly **$11.50 to $14**, before any deductions for non-metal parts. That's our math, not a quote. Scrap prices change daily and vary by yard. Call ahead, bring a photo ID (Sahd requires one), and ask whether they take refrigerant appliances and what they charge.

## 5. Donate a working appliance

Habitat for Humanity ReStores generally take household appliances that are **less than 10 years old, in good working order, with all their parts**. Some locations offer free pickup of large items ([Habitat for Humanity](https://www.habitat.org/restore/should-i-donate-habitat-restore)). Phoenix points residents to a local nonprofit (Stardust) that picks up working appliances ([City of Phoenix](https://www.phoenix.gov/administration/departments/publicworks/residential-trash-recycling/residential-recycling/appliance-recycling.html)). Call your local store first. Acceptance varies by location and season.

One counterpoint: EPA says reselling old, inefficient fridges and freezers "should be avoided to save energy" ([U.S. EPA](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal)). For a 20-year-old garage fridge, a utility recycling program is often the better route.

## 7. Dumpsters: usually not for fridges

Dumpsters.com says refrigerators, freezers, and AC units can't go in its dumpsters **unless a professional removes the Freon**. It lists refrigerators and freezers, air conditioners and dehumidifiers, water coolers and ice makers, microwaves, and dishwashers and washing machines with digital panels as appliances to ask about first. Some areas allow them for an extra fee. Hot water tanks are never allowed. If you toss a prohibited item without telling them, you may get a prohibited item fee or a failed-trip fee ([Dumpsters.com: Prohibited items](https://www.dumpsters.com/resources/prohibited-items)).

Per-item appliance and Freon surcharges from other haulers are listed in [How much does a dumpster rental cost?](/how-much-does-a-dumpster-rental-cost/). ## Which option should I pick?

| Your situation | Try first | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Buying a new appliance with delivery | Retailer haul-away at checkout | City pickup or junk removal if the retailer won't take it |
| Working fridge or freezer, not replacing it | Your electric utility's recycling program | Donation (if under 10 years old), then city Freon pickup |
| Dead fridge or freezer | City Freon appointment or sticker program | Junk removal, or a scrap yard that recovers refrigerant |
| Washer, dryer, stove, dishwasher | City bulky pickup or a scrap yard | Junk removal |
| Several appliances plus other junk | Junk removal (one visit) | Dumpster for the non-appliance junk |

## Prep checklist before any pickup

1. **Empty it.** Food, clothes, dishes, and personal items out. Best Buy, Lowe's, and Home Depot all require this.
2. **Unplug and defrost** the fridge a few days ahead.
3. **Disconnect** water, gas, and hardwired power, or hire a pro. Retail crews and junk crews generally won't do it.
4. **Tape doors shut or remove them** if the unit will sit outside.
5. **Measure the path** (doorways, stairs) and tell the crew about stairs. It can change the price.
6. **Ask where it goes.** Angi suggests asking a retailer what happens to the old fridge ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-dispose-of-refrigerator.htm)).

## FAQ

**Is it illegal to put a refrigerator in the trash?** Federal rules put the duty on the final processor (the landfill or scrap recycler) to make sure refrigerant is recovered ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/section608/stationary-refrigeration-safe-disposal-requirements)). Your city may have its own rules too, like a sticker, certificate, or appointment. Don't just set a fridge at the curb. Check your city's rules first.

**Can I remove the Freon myself?** EPA says people recovering refrigerant from small appliances in the waste stream don't have to be Section 608 certified, but the recovery equipment must meet the same performance standards as service equipment ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/section608/stationary-refrigeration-safe-disposal-requirements)). In practice, most homeowners don't own that equipment. Hire a pro or use a city or utility program.

**Will a scrap yard pay me for a refrigerator?** Some will, after refrigerant recovery, and some charge for it (one Pennsylvania yard charges $10 a unit). Others refuse fridges entirely. Call first.

**Does Best Buy take an appliance I didn't buy there?** Its $199.99 Standalone Haul-Away doesn't require a purchase and covers up to 2 large products ([Best Buy](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/recycling/tv-appliances-haul-away/pcmcat174900050009.c?id=pcmcat174900050009)).

**Do junk removal companies take appliances?** Most do, often with a fee for refrigerant appliances. See [What junk removal companies won't take](/what-junk-removal-companies-wont-take/) for each hauler's limits.

**Why do washers cost more to remove than fridges in Angi's table?** Angi doesn't give a reason. It lists washers and dryers at $100–$250 and refrigerators at $50–$150, and notes that weight, access, and disconnection affect price ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/appliance-removal-cost.htm)). Get a quote for your specific unit.
